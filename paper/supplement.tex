\section{Proofs}\label{sec:proofs}
Throughout, Assumption~\ref{M-as:split} holds: the rows are split uniformly at random, so given the set of training rows the other rows are assigned to calibration and test at random, and the seen/new label $\kappa_T$ is a fixed function of the training features.

\paragraph{Equation~(\ref{M-eq:decomp}).} By the law of total probability, $c=\pi c_{\mathrm S}+(1-\pi)c_{\mathrm N}$; solving for $c_{\mathrm N}$ gives the result. Split conformal prediction gives $1-\alpha\le c\le 1-\alpha+1/(n_C+1)$ when scores have no ties (as in \cite{M-lei2018}). Hence, with $m_{\mathrm S}=1-c_{\mathrm S}$, the miss rate on new cases $m_{\mathrm N}=1-c_{\mathrm N}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\frac{\alpha-1/(n_C+1)-\pi m_{\mathrm S}}{1-\pi}\le m_{\mathrm N}\le \frac{\alpha-\pi m_{\mathrm S}}{1-\pi}.
\end{equation}
Exact duplicates in the calibration set create tied scores; ties keep the lower bound on coverage but can break the upper one.

\paragraph{Equation~(\ref{M-eq:exposure}) (standard combinatorics).} Given that row $j$ is a test row, the training set is a uniformly random $n_T$-subset of the other $N-1$ rows. Row $j$ is new exactly when none of its $m_j-1$ copies is chosen, which has probability $\binom{N-m_j}{n_T}/\binom{N-1}{n_T}$. Averaging over rows gives Eq.~(\ref{M-eq:exposure}).

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{M-prop:pure} (closed-form label-pure exposure).} Let row $j$ have $m_j-1$ copies, $d_j$ of which carry a different label. Row $j$ is label-pure seen if no differently labelled copy is chosen for training but at least one copy is. The event ``no differently labelled copy chosen'' has probability $\binom{N-1-d_j}{n_T}/\binom{N-1}{n_T}$ and contains the event ``no copy chosen'', of probability $\binom{N-m_j}{n_T}/\binom{N-1}{n_T}$. The difference is the probability of the event of interest; averaging over rows gives Eq.~(\ref{M-eq:pure}). \emph{Check:} the closed form agreed with an average over 30 random splits to within 0.005 on five data sets (kc1, pc1, analcatdata\_supreme, black\_friday, wine\_quality), and the tool's unit test confirms it by simulation. \qed

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{M-prop:gstar} (memorization limit).} Call a row memorizable if its training copies all carry exactly its label, and let $\pi_P$ be the probability that a calibration or test row is memorizable. Under Assumptions~\ref{M-as:mem} and~\ref{M-as:equal}, calibration scores are draws from the mixture $\pi_P\delta_0+(1-\pi_P)F$ with $F$ continuous. The conformal threshold is an empirical quantile at level $\lceil(n_C+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/n_C\to1-\alpha$; by the law of large numbers for empirical quantiles it converges to the $(1-\alpha)$-quantile of the mixture. If $\pi_P<1-\alpha$, this is the point $t$ with $\pi_P+(1-\pi_P)F(t)=1-\alpha$, so a non-memorizable row (in particular a new row) is covered with probability $F(t)=1-\alpha/(1-\pi_P)$, a gap of $-\alpha\pi_P/(1-\pi_P)$. If $\pi_P\ge1-\alpha$ the threshold is $0$ and non-memorizable rows are covered with probability $F(0)=0$. In a simulation with $\pi_P=0.4$ and 1,000 calibration rows (800 repetitions), the coverage of non-memorizable rows was within 0.01 of $1-0.1/0.6$ (unit test \texttt{test\_purity\_limit\_simulation}). \qed

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{M-prop:mondrian} (validity of separate calibration; standard).} Let $\hat q_k$ be the $\lceil(n_k+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th smallest score among the $n_k$ calibration rows of group $k$ ($+\infty$ if that rank exceeds $n_k$). Condition on $T$, on the group of test row $j$ and on the multiset of group-$k$ rows in $C\cup\{j\}$. Because $\kappa_T$ is fixed given $T$ and the remaining rows are exchangeable, each of these $n_k+1$ rows is equally likely to be the test row, so the rank of the test score among them is uniform and $\Pr\{Y_j\in\mathcal C(X_j)\mid\kappa_T(X_j)=k\}\ge1-\alpha$ (the Mondrian argument of \cite{M-vovk2022}). Any deployment mix of seen and new cases is a weighted average of two quantities that are each at least $1-\alpha$. \qed

\paragraph{Safety margin (standard).} With continuous scores, the coverage of the threshold at rank $r$ among $n$ calibration scores has the Beta$(r,n-r+1)$ distribution over calibration draws. Choosing the smallest $r$ with $\Pr\{\mathrm{Beta}(r,n-r+1)<1-\alpha\}\le\delta$ gives coverage at least $1-\alpha$ with probability at least $1-\delta$ \cite{M-vovk2012}.

\section{Hypothesis register}\label{sec:register}
