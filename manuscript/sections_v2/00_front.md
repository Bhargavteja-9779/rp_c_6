---
title: "Duplicate Data Rows Make Prediction Uncertainty Look Better Than It Is: A Simple Check and Fix for New Cases"
---

**Abstract** Conformal prediction wraps a machine-learning model so that its prediction sets contain the true answer at a promised rate, for example 90% of the time. Practitioners usually confirm this promise on a random test split. Tabular data, however, often contain repeated rows, and a random split then places copies of many test rows in the training data. The check averages over these easy repeats and genuinely new cases, so it can report 90% while new cases, the ones that matter after deployment, are covered far less often. We propose a three-step routine: check, fix, report. The check counts repeated rows and whether they agree on the label, and predicts the worst-case shortfall before any model is trained. The fix calibrates seen and new cases separately, which restores the promised coverage for new cases. The report states coverage on new cases next to the usual figure. In software-defect prediction, a random forest's sets reported {{cs_mc1_reported}}% coverage but covered only {{cs_mc1_new}}% of new modules; after the fix, {{cs_mc1_fixed}}%. Similar shortfalls appeared in phishing and network-intrusion data. Across {{n_total}} benchmark data sets the problem was usually small, and the check said so. On {{pr_n}} fresh data sets analysed under a plan fixed in advance, the fix kept new-case coverage on target and the check separated safe from risky data, although it did not rank data sets by severity. The routine takes seconds and needs no change to the model.

**Keywords** Conformal prediction · Uncertainty quantification · Duplicate records · Data leakage · Software defect prediction · Tabular data
