---
title: "Seen or new? Duplicate records, conformal coverage on novel inputs, and a novelty-conditional remedy for tabular benchmarks"
---

**Abstract** Split conformal prediction guarantees that its prediction sets reach a chosen coverage level, but only on average over held-out rows. When a tabular dataset contains repeated records, a random split places exact copies of some test rows in the training set, and the guarantee then averages over rows the model has already seen and genuinely new ones. We show that coverage on new (novel) records equals $(c-\pi c_{\mathrm S})/(1-\pi)$, where $\pi$ is the share of test rows with a training copy and $c_{\mathrm S}$ the coverage of those rows. When seen rows are always covered, the error rate on new records inflates from $\alpha$ to $\alpha/(1-\pi)$. We give a label-free formula that predicts $\pi$ from duplicate-group sizes, and we prove that a novelty-Mondrian conformal predictor is valid for any mix of seen and new records. Across {{n_total}} OpenML-CC18/CTR23 datasets, five learners and four scores, predicted and observed exposure agreed closely (mean absolute error {{exp_mae}}). The pre-specified primary hypothesis that the typical duplicated dataset under-covers new records was not supported: median gaps were {{h1x_gap_median_min}} to {{h1x_gap_median_max}} percentage points, excluding a tie-inflated k-NN variant. Shortfalls were concentrated where exposure was high and seen records were over-covered, reaching {{h1_worst_gap}} points, and they were reproduced in external defect-prediction and intrusion-detection data and, as a dose–response, by controlled duplicate injection. Novelty-Mondrian calibration restored nominal coverage on new records at a median set-size cost of at most {{h3_novel_max_pct}}%; in regression, a distance-normalized score also removed most of the shortfall, but without a guarantee.

**Keywords** Conformal prediction · Uncertainty quantification · Data leakage · Duplicate records · Tabular benchmarks · Mondrian calibration
