# Results

All runs completed: {{n_total}} datasets × 10 splits × 2 split protocols × 5 learners, with no failed fits. Every number below is computed by the released analysis code from the per-split result files.

## Duplicates are common, and exposure is predictable from a label-free audit

Of the {{n_total}} datasets, {{n_any_dup}} contain at least one duplicated feature vector. In {{n_dup_ge1}}, at least 1% of rows belong to a duplicate group, and in {{n_dup_ge30}} at least 30% do. In {{n_conflict}} datasets some duplicate groups carry conflicting labels. The confirmatory set DUP (predicted exposure $\bar\pi\ge 0.01$) contains {{n_dup_cls}} classification and {{n_dup_reg}} regression datasets, with median predicted exposure {{dup_exposure_median}} (maximum {{dup_exposure_max}}). In {{n_dup_exposure_ge01}} of them, at least 10% of test rows are expected to have an exact training copy, and in {{n_dup_exposure_ge03}} at least 30%. The CLEAN null-control set contains {{n_clean_cls}} classification and {{n_clean_reg}} regression datasets, and {{n_low}} datasets have negligible exposure. The audit statistics for every dataset are listed in Online Resource 1.

Proposition 2 predicts the exposure observed under random splitting almost exactly (Fig. 1b). The mean absolute error between predicted and observed exposure (the latter averaged over the 10 splits) is {{exp_mae}}, the maximum error is {{exp_maxerr}}, and the Pearson correlation is {{exp_r}}. A dataset's exposure can therefore be read off before any model is trained.

## Pre-registered test: is novel-record coverage below nominal across duplicated datasets?

Table 2 summarizes the primary analysis at $\alpha=0.1$. Averaged over datasets, standard split CP is close to nominal on novel records. Median gaps range from {{h1_gap_median_min}} to {{h1_gap_median_max}} percentage points (pp) across the {{n_families}} task × learner × score families, and **none of the one-sided tests of H1 is significant after Holm correction** (smallest adjusted $p$ = {{h1_min_p_holm}}). The pre-registered primary hypothesis, that the *typical* duplicated benchmark dataset shows novel-record under-coverage, is therefore not supported.

The distribution of gaps is, however, strongly skewed. In every family the worst dataset under-covers novel records, by up to {{h1_worst_gap_cls}} pp in classification and {{h1_worst_gap_reg}} pp in regression. Between {{h1_share_lt1_range}}% of DUP datasets per family fall short by more than 1 pp. As H1a predicts, the gap grows with exposure: Spearman correlations between exposure and gap range from {{h1a_rho_range}}, and they are negative and significant ($p<0.05$) in {{h1a_n_neg_sig}} of {{n_families}} families (Fig. 2). Overall coverage stays at nominal throughout (Table 2 and Online Resource 1), so the shortfall on novel records is invisible in the coverage that is normally reported.

The exception is $k$-NN with the LAC score, whose novel coverage is above nominal (median {{knn_lac_novel_median}}). A 5-NN classifier outputs class probabilities in multiples of 0.2, so its scores are heavily tied, which makes split CP conservative (see the null control below). The Friedman test across learners (H1b) is significant for LAC ($\chi^2$ = {{fr_classification_lac_chi2}}, $p$ {{fr_classification_lac_p}}) and APS ($\chi^2$ = {{fr_classification_aps_chi2}}, $p$ = {{fr_classification_aps_p}}), but not for regression ($\chi^2$ = {{fr_regression_absres_chi2}}, $p$ = {{fr_regression_absres_p}}). For LAC the difference is driven by this tie effect rather than by memorization capacity. H1b is therefore better tested by the capacity experiment below, which varies flexibility within a learner.

## Where the guarantee fails, and why (exploratory)

These analyses were added after the primary result and are labelled exploratory (Online Resource 1). Proposition 1 implies that exposure is necessary but not sufficient for a gap: seen records must also be over-covered. Table 3 stratifies the {{x_pairs}} dataset × learner × score combinations of the DUP set by exposure. Below $\pi=0.1$ ({{x_lo_ds}} datasets), no combination falls short by more than 2 pp. For $0.1\le\pi<0.3$, {{x_mid_share2}}% of combinations do, and for $\pi\ge 0.3$, {{x_hi_share2}}% fall short by more than 2 pp and {{x_hi_share5}}% by more than 5 pp. In total, {{x_pairs_lt2}} combinations from {{x_ds_lt2}} datasets fall short by more than 2 pp, and {{x_pairs_lt5}} combinations from {{x_ds_lt5}} datasets by more than 5 pp.

The decomposition of Proposition 1 shows which datasets fail (Fig. 8). The seen-coverage term $-\pi(c_{\mathrm S}-(1-\alpha))/(1-\pi)$ is strongly associated with the observed gap (Spearman $\rho$ = {{x_rho_mech}}, against $\rho$ = {{x_rho_exp}} for exposure alone). The remaining marginal term is small (median absolute value {{x_marginal}} pp). The two most exposed datasets illustrate the point. In *solar_flare* ($\pi\approx 0.85$), seen records are covered at {{sf_seen_range}} and novel coverage falls to {{sf_novel_range}} across learners. In *cps88wages*, which has the same exposure but conflicting labels in almost every duplicate group, seen records are covered at the nominal rate ({{cps_seen_range}}) and novel records do not fall short ({{cps_novel_range}}). Large gaps also occur where duplicates mostly carry consistent labels, as in the NASA defect data (*kc1*: seen {{kc1_seen_range}}, novel {{kc1_novel_range}}), *breast-w* and the *Internet-Advertisements* data.

## Controlled experiments

**Duplicate injection (dose–response).** Injecting exact copies into the {{inj_n_ds}} duplicate-free datasets with at least 1,000 rows raised the exposure as Proposition 2 predicts. At duplication rates $\rho$ = 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 and 1, median exposure was {{inj_classification_rf_0p1_pi}}, {{inj_classification_rf_0p25_pi}}, {{inj_classification_rf_0p5_pi}} and {{inj_classification_rf_1_pi}}. The novel-record gap of split CP grew monotonically for the flexible learners (Fig. 4). For random forests, the median gap went from {{inj_classification_rf_0_gap}} pp at $\rho=0$ to {{inj_classification_rf_0p5_gap}} pp at $\rho=0.5$ and {{inj_classification_rf_1_gap}} pp at $\rho=1$ in classification ({{inj_regression_rf_1_gap}} pp in regression), against a memorization limit of {{inj_classification_rf_1_lim}} pp. For LightGBM it reached {{inj_classification_lgbm_1_gap}} pp (classification) and {{inj_regression_lgbm_1_gap}} pp (regression). Regularized linear models were barely affected ({{inj_classification_logreg_1_gap}} and {{inj_regression_logreg_1_gap}} pp), because their seen records are not over-covered. The within-dataset Spearman correlation between $\rho$ and the gap was negative for RF (median {{inj_classification_rf_rho}}, $p$ {{inj_classification_rf_p}}; regression {{inj_regression_rf_rho}}, $p$ {{inj_regression_rf_p}}) and for LightGBM (median {{inj_classification_lgbm_rho}}, $p$ {{inj_classification_lgbm_p}}; regression {{inj_regression_lgbm_rho}}, $p$ {{inj_regression_lgbm_p}}). Novelty-Mondrian CP stayed at nominal at every rate (RF at $\rho=1$: {{inj_classification_rf_1_mcp}} and {{inj_regression_rf_1_mcp}} pp). For $k$-NN classification, tied scores make split CP conservative at baseline ({{inj_classification_knn_0_gap}} pp), and injection erodes this margin ({{inj_classification_knn_1_gap}} pp at $\rho=1$).

![**Fig. 4** Controlled duplicate injection into duplicate-free datasets: median novel-record coverage gap (coverage − 0.90) versus injected duplication rate, for split CP (solid) and novelty-Mondrian CP (dotted)](Fig4.png)

**Model capacity.** On the {{cap_n_ds}} benchmark datasets with $\bar\pi\ge 0.05$, reducing flexibility lowered the coverage of seen records, as expected. For random forests the median went from {{cap_classification_rf_0_seen}} (leaf size 1) to {{cap_classification_rf_3_seen}} (leaf size 100) in classification, and from {{cap_regression_rf_0_seen}} to {{cap_regression_rf_3_seen}} in regression. The corresponding change in the novel-record gap was too small to detect, however. Median gaps were {{cap_classification_rf_0_gap}} versus {{cap_classification_rf_3_gap}} pp (classification) and {{cap_regression_rf_0_gap}} versus {{cap_regression_rf_3_gap}} pp (regression), and the within-dataset trend tests were not significant (RF: $p$ = {{cap_classification_rf_p}} and {{cap_regression_rf_p}}; linear: $p$ = {{cap_classification_logreg_p}} and {{cap_regression_logreg_p}}). At the moderate exposures of these datasets (median about 0.12–0.20), Proposition 1 predicts differences below 1 pp. The capacity half of H5 is therefore **not supported** by this experiment. The learner differences in the injection experiment, where flexible ensembles were affected far more than regularized linear models, are the stronger evidence for the role of capacity. For $k$-NN the experiment is uninformative: with $k=1$ the predicted probabilities are 0 or 1, the scores are almost all tied, and split CP returns near-trivial sets (median gap {{cap_classification_knn_0_gap}} pp). Fig. 5 shows the results.

## Remedies

**Novelty-Mondrian CP (H2, H3).** MCP-R restores nominal coverage on novel records. Its median gap lies between {{h2_mcp_median_range}} pp across families, and the bootstrap confidence interval of the median lies above the −1 pp non-inferiority margin in {{h2_noninf_all}} families (smallest lower bound {{h2_mcp_cilo_min}} pp). It improves on SCP-R significantly after Holm correction in {{h2_n_sig}} of {{n_families}} families (Table 2). Its worst dataset-level gap is {{x_worst_mcp}} pp, against {{x_worst}} pp for SCP-R. That worst case (solar_flare) reflects sampling noise from only about 36 novel calibration rows per split: in a verification run with 200 further splits, MCP-R novel coverage was 0.909 (95% CI 0.899–0.918) for LightGBM and 0.907 (0.896–0.917) for ridge, while SCP-R reached 0.78 and 0.77. The trivial-set fallback was never triggered ({{h2_fallbacks}} dataset-level occurrences). The price is small. Median sets or intervals for novel records grow by a factor of {{h3_novel_ratio_range}}, and the overall average changes by a factor of {{h3_all_ratio_range}}, because seen records receive tighter sets.

**Group-aware splitting (H6).** When whole record groups are assigned to one partition, every test row is novel by construction, and split CP attains median coverage {{h6_median_range_noknn}} (excluding the tie-inflated $k$-NN/LAC combination). Non-inferiority at −1 pp holds in {{h6_noninf}} families (Online Resource 1). Group splitting answers a different question, however. It estimates coverage for new records only, and it changes the training set. Novelty-Mondrian CP keeps the standard split and is valid for any mix of seen and new records (Corollary 1).

**Null control.** On the duplicate-free datasets, split CP is at nominal coverage (median {{null_median_range}}; $k$-NN/LAC {{null_knn_lac}} because of tied scores), and MCP-R is identical to SCP-R (largest absolute difference {{null_maxdiff}}), as it must be when no row is seen.

**Secondary miscoverage levels.** At $\alpha=0.05$ the median SCP-R gaps range from {{a0.05_gap_median_range}} pp (worst {{a0.05_worst}} pp; {{a0.05_n_sig}} families significant). At $\alpha=0.2$ they range from {{a0.2_gap_median_range}} pp (worst {{a0.2_worst}} pp; {{a0.2_n_sig}} significant). The smallest lower confidence bound of the MCP-R median gap is {{a0.05_mcp_cilo_min}} pp and {{a0.2_mcp_cilo_min}} pp, respectively.

## Calibration of predicted probabilities (H4)

Classifiers are markedly more accurate on seen than on novel test records (median accuracy {{cal_acc_seen_range}} versus {{cal_acc_novel_range}} across learners; Table 4). Overconfidence on novel records exceeds overconfidence on all records in {{cal_oc_n_sig}} of 5 learners after Holm correction. Novel-record ECE exceeds size-matched ECE in {{cal_ece_n_sig}} of 5. The median differences are small, however ({{cal_oc_diff_range}} pp for overconfidence and {{cal_ece_diff_range}} pp for ECE). H4 is supported statistically but with small effect sizes: the large accuracy gap between seen and novel records is largely matched by lower confidence on novel records (Fig. 6).

## Near-duplicates (exploratory)

Coverage of split CP also varies with the distance of a novel record to its nearest training record (Fig. 7). For novel records in the nearest distance quintile, median coverage is {{dist_classification_q1}} (classification, LAC) and {{dist_regression_q1}} (regression). In the farthest quintile it is {{dist_classification_q5}} and {{dist_regression_q5}}. Seen records are covered at {{dist_classification_seen}} and {{dist_regression_seen}}. A six-bin Mondrian variant built on the same training-only distance bins keeps every bin's median coverage at or above {{dcp_classification_min}} (classification) and {{dcp_regression_min}} (regression). Exact duplication is therefore the extreme case of a more general dependence of coverage on novelty, which difficulty-based Mondrian calibration (Boström and Johansson 2020) addresses.
