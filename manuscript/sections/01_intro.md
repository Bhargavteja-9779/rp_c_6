# Introduction

Conformal prediction (CP) has become a standard way to attach uncertainty to the predictions of arbitrary machine-learning models. Given a model trained on one part of the data and a held-out calibration set, split CP returns a prediction set (classification) or an interval (regression) that contains the true label with probability at least $1-\alpha$. The guarantee holds in finite samples and requires only exchangeability (Papadopoulos et al. 2002; Lei et al. 2018; Vovk et al. 2022; Angelopoulos and Bates 2023). Methods are evaluated in the same way the guarantee is stated: rows of a benchmark dataset are randomly split into training, calibration and test parts, and the empirical coverage on the test rows is compared with $1-\alpha$.

Rows are not always distinct records. Tabular benchmarks contain repeated feature vectors, because the same event was logged twice, because the same entity was measured repeatedly, or because a coarse discrete feature space makes identical profiles recur. A recent audit (Li 2026, a preprint) found duplicated rows in about half of the datasets in widely used tabular benchmark collections, which our own audit confirms ({{n_any_dup}} of {{n_total}} datasets), and duplication is a documented quality problem of well-known datasets such as the NASA software-defect data (Gray et al. 2011; Shepperd et al. 2013). Under a random row split, some test rows then have exact copies in the training set. Duplicate leakage of this kind inflates accuracy and can change model rankings (Li 2026; Roth 2026; both preprints), but its effect on *uncertainty quantification* has not, to our knowledge, been examined. That effect is of a different kind. CP is valued because its coverage is *guaranteed*, and practitioners use the guarantee to make risk-sensitive decisions. If the reported 90% is an average over records the model has already seen and records it has not, the guarantee for new records, which are what a deployed model mostly encounters, may be weaker than the one reported.

This paper asks whether that happens, by how much, where, and how to prevent it. Our contributions are:

1. **An exact characterization.** Coverage on novel records equals $(c-\pi c_{\mathrm S})/(1-\pi)$, where $\pi$ is the share of test rows with a training copy and $c_{\mathrm S}$ their coverage. Novel records are under-covered exactly when seen records are over-covered. In the memorization limit the error rate on novel records is $\alpha/(1-\pi)$ (Proposition 1).
2. **A label-free exposure formula** that predicts $\pi$ from the sizes of duplicate groups, before any model is trained (Proposition 2).
3. **A remedy with a guarantee.** Calibrating seen and novel records separately is exactly valid for both groups, and therefore for any deployment mix of seen and new records (Proposition 3 and Corollary 1). The method is standard Mondrian CP (Vovk et al. 2022); what is new is the taxonomy, which is defined by membership of the training set, and the argument that this data-dependent taxonomy is admissible.
4. **A large-scale evaluation following a pre-specified protocol** (committed to version control before the full runs, not lodged in an external registry) on {{n_total}} datasets of the OpenML-CC18 and OpenML-CTR23 suites, with five learners, four nonconformity scores, three miscoverage levels, a group-aware split, controlled duplicate-injection and model-capacity experiments, an analysis of probability calibration, and an external validation on defect-prediction and intrusion-detection data with documented duplication.

The evidence supports a more nuanced conclusion than the pre-specified primary hypothesis. For the *typical* duplicated benchmark dataset, coverage on novel records is within about 1 percentage point of nominal, and the primary test was not significant. The shortfall is concentrated in datasets with high exposure whose seen records are over-covered. There it reaches {{h1_worst_gap}} points in the benchmark suites and {{ext_scp_min}} points in the external data, as the theory predicts. By contrast, a dataset with equally high exposure but conflicting duplicate labels (cps88wages) was unaffected. Because both quantities can be audited cheaply, at-risk datasets can be identified in advance, and novelty-Mondrian calibration removes the problem at a small cost in set size. The pipeline, raw results and analysis code are released.
